        Model
        
    
          
    u̇ = u · (φ(v) − α)     φ(v) = −γ + ρ v
    
            v̇ = v · ((1 − u) / σ − α − β)
    
          
    
            
    u
    wage share of output (workers' share), so 1 − u is the profit share
    
            
    v
    employment rate (employed labour ÷ labour force)
    
            
    α, β
    growth rates of labour productivity and of the labour force
    
            
    σ
    capital–output ratio; all profits are invested, so output grows at (1 − u)/σ
    
            
    φ(v)
    linear Phillips curve: real-wage growth rises with employment (intercept −γ, slope ρ)
    
          
    
          
    Equilibrium
    
          
    v* = (α + γ) / ρ     u* = 1 − σ (α + β)
    
          
    An interior equilibrium needs u* > 0 and v* > 0. The model itself does not keep u and v
            below 1; values above 1 are economically meaningless and flagged as warnings.
    
          
    Cycles
    
          
    The system is of Lotka–Volterra type: employment plays the prey and the wage share the predator.
            High employment pushes wages up, the profit share falls, accumulation and employment slow, wages
            then lag, profits recover, and the cycle repeats. The quantity
    
          
    H(u, v) = u/σ − a ln u + ρ v − c ln v,   a = 1/σ − α − β,   c = α + γ
    
          
    is constant along every trajectory, so orbits are closed curves around (v*, u*): neither damped
            nor explosive, with amplitude set by the initial state. Near the equilibrium the period is
    
          
    T ≈ 2π / √(a · c)
    
          
    and larger orbits take somewhat longer.
